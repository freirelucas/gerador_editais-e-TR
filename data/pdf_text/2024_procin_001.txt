           RESEARCH FELLOWSHIP GRANT - TERMS OF REFERENCE

1. PROJECT TITLE

"Business Environment and Competitiveness."

2. RESEARCH PURPOSE

The low growth of Brazilian productivity in recent decades has trapped us in the so-
called "middle-income trap." This term was coined by Gill, Kharas, and Bhattasali
(2007) to characterize economies that, after reaching intermediate income levels, had
more difficulty continuing to grow. The continuity of economic development, after
reaching a certain income level, achieved after the industrialization of these countries,
would      require    more      sophisticated   institutions   and     public    policies.
Economists like Acemoglu and Robinson (2012) argue that the quality of institutions—
understood as the "rules of the game" that organize and limit political, social, and
economic behavior—is crucial in determining a nation's economic success or failure.
The proper functioning of institutions would play a significant role in several aspects
crucial for economic development: resource allocation; investment and
entrepreneurship incentives; uncertainty reduction; promotion of competition; income
distribution; and universal access to public services, such as electricity and sanitation.
Institutions, therefore, directly influence the business environment, determining the
incentives and constraints that shape the behavior of companies and investors.
The research aims to produce an academic article, to be published as a chapter in an
Ipea book. The specific article should analyze how the business environment in Brazil
is one of the constraints on the country's aggregate productivity growth.
The business environment in Brazil has long been pointed out as one of the limitations
to the country's economic development and the competitiveness of its companies,
generating excessive costs for economic activity, sometimes called the "Brazil cost."
Mation (2014) showed, through indicators calculated by the World Bank in "Doing
Business," that the indicators for the Brazilian economy remained modest in the last
two decades, with improvements restricted to a few indicators. Cavalcante (2015)
estimated the effects of improvements in the business environment on the level of
gross fixed capital formation in the country. In a world where most countries have
shown significant improvements in their business costs, Brazil's slow evolution has
worsened                       its                    relative                   position.
The current moment is conducive to accelerating regulatory reforms that can decisively
contribute to improving the institutional conditions for doing business in the Brazilian
economy. This year, 2023, the country has already taken some important steps
towards developing the competitiveness agenda. The most recent and perhaps most
important is the tax reform, in the final stages of debate in the National Congress. The
complexity of the Brazilian tax structure is one aspect frequently mentioned by
companies as detrimental to their competitiveness. In addition to reducing the system's
complexity, the reform has the potential to make it more progressive and fairer,
contributing to reducing Brazil's immense social inequalities, one of the main barriers to
the                                 country's                               development.
In this sense, the book to be produced by Ipea can significantly contribute to the debate
on the necessary regulatory reforms in the Brazilian economy.
